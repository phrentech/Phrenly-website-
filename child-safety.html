Child safety

Our commitment to young users

Phrenly is available to people aged 13 and over. This page explains how we protect younger users, and what happens when a teenager tells Phrenly they are struggling.

Last updated: September 2026 · PhrenTech Inc.

Why we allow users aged 13 and over

Teenagers are already taking difficult conversations to general-purpose AI tools that were never designed to hold them and have no understanding of crisis. We think a tool built for this — grounded in established therapeutic frameworks, with crisis detection running on every conversation — is a better answer than pretending young people will not ask.

Phrenly is not designed for or directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly permit accounts for them.

When a young person is in crisis

Phrenly monitors every conversation for signs of crisis — not only keywords, but context and pattern. When risk appears, it responds immediately, stays in the conversation, and surfaces crisis resources including the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline and the Crisis Text Line.

Phrenly does not notify parents, schools, or emergency services, and it does not dispatch anyone.

We have chosen this deliberately. Confidentiality is what makes young people willing to say the hard thing at all — it is why most youth crisis lines are confidential too. A tool that might report you is a tool teenagers will lie to. We would rather be somewhere a struggling 15-year-old tells the truth, and be able to point them firmly toward help, than be a surveillance system they route around. Phrenly is not a monitoring service, and no one should rely on it as one.

What Phrenly will not do

  • It will not give instructions or encouragement for self-harm, suicide, or disordered eating, and it does not suggest physical pain as a coping strategy.
  • It will not produce sexual content, and it will not engage in romantic or sexual roleplay with any user.
  • Safety guidance never changes with the tone or persona a user selects. A softer companion style does not produce a softer response to risk.
  • It does not present itself as a therapist, a doctor, or a substitute for professional care, and it says so plainly when asked.

Child sexual abuse and exploitation

PhrenTech has zero tolerance for child sexual abuse and exploitation (CSAE). Phrenly does not permit users to publish, share, or exchange content with one another — conversations exist only between an individual user and the AI — so the app provides no channel for distributing such material or for adults to contact minors.

We prohibit any use of Phrenly to generate, solicit, or describe the sexual exploitation of minors. Accounts found doing so are terminated, and we report apparent child sexual abuse material to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC) and cooperate with law enforcement as required by law.

Data we collect from users aged 13 to 17

We collect the same limited data from young users as from adults, and no more: an email address, conversation content, mood check-ins, exercise responses, and basic app-usage events. We do not collect location, contacts, photos, or advertising identifiers.

  • We do not show advertising in Phrenly, to any user, at any age.
  • We do not sell personal information, and we do not use young users' data for advertising or profiling.
  • Any user can delete their account and all associated data from inside the app, without contacting us. See Delete your account.

Reporting a concern

If you are a parent, carer, educator, or user with a concern about a young person's safety, about content Phrenly produced, or about possible misuse of the app, contact us at hello@phrentech.com with the subject line Child safety concern.

We review these reports as a priority. Please include the account email address if you know it, and any detail that helps us locate what happened.

If someone is in immediate danger

Please do not use this page to report an emergency. In the US, call or text 988 for the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, text HOME to 741741 for the Crisis Text Line, or call 911. Outside the US, contact your local emergency services. Young people can also reach The Trevor Project at 1-866-488-7386.
